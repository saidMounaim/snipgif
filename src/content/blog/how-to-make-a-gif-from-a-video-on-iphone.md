You filmed something funny, or you have a clip from a game or a screen recording, and you want it as a GIF you can drop into a chat. On an iPhone you don't need to install an app for that. Snipgif runs in Safari and does the conversion on your phone itself, so the video is never uploaded anywhere.

Here is the whole process, from the video in your Photos to a GIF you can send.

## What you need

- An iPhone with Safari (or any other browser).
- The video saved in your Photos or in the Files app. MP4, MOV, WebM, and AVI all work, and videos recorded with the iPhone camera are MOV or MP4 files, so they are covered.

There is no account to create and nothing to pay. The GIF has no watermark.

## Step by step

1. Open Snipgif in Safari. The video to GIF converter is on the home page.
2. Tap the drop zone. iOS asks where the video should come from: choose **Photo Library** to pick a clip from Photos, or **Choose File** to pick one from Files.
3. Pick the part you want. Drag the **Start** and **End** handles on the timeline while the preview shows you the frame. A GIF can be up to 30 seconds long, but short clips of 2 to 6 seconds look best and stay small.
4. Choose a preset. **Small for chat** gives the smallest file, **Balanced** is a good default, and **High quality** keeps more detail at a larger size. You can also set the frame rate, width, and quality yourself.
5. Optionally crop, change the speed, or add a caption. Crop to **1:1** for a square post or **9:16** for a story, play the clip faster or in reverse, make it loop back and forth, or add meme-style text.
6. Tap **Convert to GIF**. The first conversion takes a little longer because the converter itself has to load. A progress bar shows how far along it is.
7. Save or send the result. Snipgif shows the finished GIF with its file size.

## Saving the GIF to your iPhone

Once the GIF is ready you have two ways to keep it:

- **Share** opens the iOS share sheet. From there you can send the GIF straight to Messages, WhatsApp, or another app, or save it to your photo library. Saved GIFs show up in the **Animated** album in Photos.
- **Download GIF** saves the file through Safari. You find it in the Files app, usually in the **Downloads** folder.

If the share sheet does not offer the app you want, download the GIF first and attach it from Files.

## Tips for a better GIF

- **Keep it short.** Every extra second adds frames, and frames add size. Most reaction GIFs are under five seconds.
- **Start with Small for chat** when the GIF is going into a messaging app. Many chat apps limit upload sizes or shrink large images, so a smaller file usually looks better once it arrives.
- **Crop out the edges.** Removing empty background lets the subject fill the frame at the same width.
- **Large videos take more memory.** Phones have less memory than computers. If a long or very large video struggles, trim it in the Photos app first, then convert the shorter clip.

## Why do it in the browser?

Most GIF apps either add a watermark, show full-screen ads, or upload your video to a server to process it. Snipgif converts on your iPhone, so your video stays private and the speed depends on your phone, not on a queue. When you close the tab, the video and the GIF are gone from the page.

If your GIF still comes out too big, run it through the [GIF compressor](/gif-compressor) to reduce colors or skip frames.
