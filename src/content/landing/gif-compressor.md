## Why GIFs get so big

GIF stores every frame as a separate image with up to 256 colors. A few seconds
of animation can add up to many megabytes, which is often too big for chat
apps, email attachments, forum posts, or a fast-loading web page.

Snipgif's compressor shrinks the file in three ways, and you can combine them.

## Three ways to shrink a GIF

- **Fewer colors.** Choose 256, 128, 64, or 32 colors. Simple graphics,
  screenshots, and cartoons often look the same with far fewer colors.
- **Skip frames.** Keep every 2nd or 3rd frame to cut the file roughly in half
  or by two thirds. Snipgif keeps the total length the same, so the animation
  plays at the same pace, just less smoothly.
- **Smaller width.** Shrinking the width makes every frame smaller. If the GIF
  will be shown small anyway, this is the change you will notice least.

## Compare before you download

After compressing, Snipgif shows the new GIF with its file size next to the
original, so you can see how much you saved. If you don't like the result,
change a setting and compress again. Your original file is never changed.
